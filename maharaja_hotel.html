Heritage & Legacy

A Palace Born of
Imperial Dreams

✦

Conceived in 1892 by the Maharaja of Udaipur as his summer residence, Maharaja Palace stands as an enduring testament to Rajput architectural brilliance. Every arch, every jaali screen, every gilded dome tells the story of a civilisation that elevated beauty to a form of devotion.

Today, the palace welcomes guests into a world where centuries of royal tradition are seamlessly woven into the fabric of contemporary luxury hospitality.

The Royal Suites

Chambers Fit for
Maharajas

✦

Each of our 84 palatial rooms and suites is a private world unto itself — adorned with hand-painted frescoes, inlaid marble floors, antique furnishings sourced from royal estates, and shimmering silk drapery in the colours of the Rajput court.

Culinary Artistry

Royal Feasts,
Ancient Recipes

✦

Dining at Maharaja Palace is an act of cultural immersion. Our master chefs draw upon closely guarded royal recipes — once prepared exclusively for the Maharaja's court — reimagining classic Rajasthani cuisine with rare spices, hand-ground masalas, and ingredients sourced from heirloom farms.

From formal fine dining beneath ornate chandeliers to lakeside candlelit dinners, each meal is a ceremony in itself.

A Royal Journey

A Story Written
in Gold & Marble

✦

There is a moment — it comes as the sun begins to dissolve into the waters of Lake Pichola, turning the sky amber and the palace walls to burnished fire — when the traveller understands that they have arrived not at a hotel, but at something older and greater: a story.

The Maharajas of Udaipur did not merely build palaces. They composed them, as a musician composes a raga — with deliberate beauty, with an understanding of light and shadow, of human longing and divine proportion. To walk these corridors is to walk through that composition.

Guests who arrive at Maharaja Palace often say the same thing: they came for a holiday and found instead a homecoming — a sense of recognition, as if some ancient part of them had always known this place, had always belonged to its courtyard gardens, its flickering oil lamps, its music drifting over still water at dusk.
